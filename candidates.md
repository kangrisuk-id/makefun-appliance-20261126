# Candidate tournament — The Funniest Honest Appliance Error

Judge criteria, in the brief's order (each scored 1–5, total /25):

1. **Honest error** — a message an appliance could plausibly show, one line ≤ 200 chars
2. **Laugh** — a real joke, surprise or twist, not just cute phrasing
3. **Honesty** — names a fault an appliance could plausibly report
4. **Machine voice** — sounds like the appliance itself, flat and put-upon
5. **Economy** — every word earns its place, no padding, no explaining the joke

Devices used across the 36: deadpan confession, futile effort, blame-the-owner,
resignation, understatement, over-specific numbers, the machine giving up,
the machine knowing more than the owner. Appliances: washer, dryer, fridge,
freezer, toaster, microwave, vacuum, robot vacuum, dishwasher, oven, boiler,
AC, printer, kettle, TV, coffee machine.

## Top 8 seeds (knockout bracket)

Seeds by total: #35 (25), #13 (24), #6 (24), #14 (24), #25 (24),
#7 (23), #17 (23), #21 (23). First reserve: #34 (23).

### Quarter-finals

- **QF1 — #35 beats #21.** C1: a leak report is a textbook washer error; a low-battery
  return is status, not a fault. C2: "I waited" reframes the whole line with a
  timing twist; the square-metre arithmetic only smiles. #35 wins at criterion 2.
- **QF2 — #13 beats #17.** C1: the turntable fault is the more literal error
  message, so #17 takes criterion 1. C2: "There never was" is a bigger, more
  universal laugh than "a box with a light", and the gap on the laugh is larger
  than the gap on criterion 1 — #13 advances.
- **QF3 — #6 beats #7.** C1 tied (both plausible faults). C2: "I remember" lands
  a menacing little twist in two words; "Nobody has asked until now" is a good
  shrug but a smaller surprise. #6 advances.
- **QF4 — #25 beats #14.** C1 tied. C2: "a cupboard with ambition" is a sharper
  image than "This is toasting", which states the joke instead of landing it.
  #25 advances.

### Semi-finals

- **SF1 — #35 beats #13.** C1: the leak is a reportable error; the toaster
  settings line is a confession, not an error report — #35 takes criterion 1.
  C2 is close (universal toaster truth vs. dark timing twist) but #35's twist
  is the bigger surprise. #35 advances.
- **SF2 — #25 beats #6.** C1 tied (coin in the pump vs. dead heating element).
  C2: "a cupboard with ambition" out-images "I remember", which leans on menace
  the machine voice cannot quite carry. #25 advances.

### Final — #35 beats #25

- C1 tied: both name a literal, reportable fault (leak / dead heating element).
- C2 decides: "The warranty expired yesterday. I waited." turns the fault into a
  timed act — the machine chose the one day nobody would pay to fix it. "A
  cupboard with ambition" is a witty self-description, but it describes the joke
  rather than springing it. #35 wins the laugh.
- C3 tied (both faults are real). C4 tied (both flat and put-upon).
- C5: #35 is 86 characters, #25 is 90; every word in #35 works, none explains.

**Winner: #35 — "Washing machine: I leaked. The floor is wet. The warranty expired yesterday. I waited." (86 chars, 25/25)**

## Why the winner is the entry

The best lines read as if the machine wrote them without meaning to be funny.
#35 does exactly that: a flat fault report (leak, wet floor) followed by a
fact (warranty expired yesterday) and a two-word verdict ("I waited.") that
turns the fault into a joke about timing. The humour comes out of the truth
of the fault — a leak the day after the warranty dies — not out of a pun.
Nothing in the line explains itself.
